\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

We tested latent extrapolation as a pretraining objective for
forecasting; at equal corpus and budget it is indistinguishable from
reconstruction (Section~\ref{sec:objective}). Given the current interest
in JEPA for time series \citep{lecun2022path,assran2023ijepa}, we state
this plainly, and report what the project delivers instead
(Figure~\ref{fig:claims}):

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A competitive small foundation model, from data alone.}
One 1.14M checkpoint, one configuration, all 97 GIFT-Eval configurations
zero-shot: \gpair{0.863}{0.596} with flip TTA (\gpair{0.895}{0.624}
plain). Every accuracy step traces to corpus composition or inference
protocol; none to the objective (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item \textbf{The frozen latent is a judge.} Its implicit energy ranks
true futures far above chance and, as a zero-training reranker, turns
TTM-R3 \citep{ekambaram2024ttm} into a probabilistic forecaster that
beats its own WQL on 6/6 held-out datasets and its point CRPS by 7\%
across GIFT (Section~\ref{sec:judge}).
\item \textbf{Four latent dimensions carry the calibration.} A residual
statistics head, gated into the quantile decoder, is load-bearing for
the predictive distribution: gate off costs $+18.7$ CRPS points with
MASE bit-identical, and what it buys the full model is nominal coverage
at equal accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:esjepa}).
\end{itemize}

Three properties frame every number: one checkpoint and one
configuration for all 97 configurations (the TTM family, among others,
publishes per-regime variants; we do not); nothing tuned or selected on
GIFT-Eval data; three RTX~3090s end to end, with predictions registered
in a dated log before each run and negatives reported as results
(Section~\ref{sec:negative}).
