\section{Corpus and batch composition}
\label{sec:corpus}

Most of the accuracy came from here. Working principle: \emph{the batch is
the target, the corpus is the means}; every decision is made against an
audit of what the sampler actually serves, never against file sizes.

\paragraph{Sources and decontamination.}
Real data is LOTSA \citep{woo2024moirai}, univariate by channel split. Every
subset matching an evaluation benchmark is excluded: 7 Nixtla patterns plus
24 GIFT patterns covering all 28 datasets, checked against the official
repository tree and pinned by a non-regression test. Exclusion is by whole
dataset; exact-name overrides (never substrings) readmit provably disjoint
subsets, each justified against all three suites. Cross-check: Salesforce's
GiftEvalPretrain is byte-identical to LOTSA minus 18 datasets; our patterns
are strictly stricter, at a quantified cost of 0.9\% of observations. PEMS
is retained and declared (same road network as \texttt{traffic}, no shared
sensor, year or frequency). We are strict because we got burned: our
original Monash corpus contained \texttt{electricity-hourly} and
\texttt{traffic-hourly}, whose last 20\% of steps \emph{are} the Nixtla
benchmark; every early number on those two is in-domain, flagged wherever
cited, and only the LOTSA lineage is presented as zero-shot
(Appendix~\ref{app:pitfalls}).

\paragraph{Chunking.}
Fixed-length non-overlapping float32 chunks (object arrays break
copy-on-write under forked workers; measured 51~GiB), length adapted per
subset, capped 2048 for real data. Chunks above 5\% missing are dropped;
below, linearly interpolated: rejecting any NaN cost 100\% of two metro
datasets whose nightly closures are regular, and a higher threshold would
fabricate 3\,a.m.\ ridership. Windows slide at stride 8 over an int32 index.
A per-family cap stops climate reanalysis (era5, cmip6: 63 of 118 audited
files) from becoming half the corpus. Short-series GIFT configurations get
a pad-to path in v3: left-pad by repeating the first value, so the real data
sits where the target is read; the target is always real. Geometry still
binds: a 256-step real target cannot come from a 30-point yearly series, so
the yearly block stays under-served. Real 5-minute data is mean-pooled by 2
and 3 into legitimate 10/15-minute series.

\paragraph{Sampler, drift, rationing.}
Batch share $\propto n_i^{0.5}$ per \emph{file}, oversampling cap
$3\times$ (at $6\times$, small files overfit visibly). Per-file weighting
cuts both ways: climate shards inflated to 66.5\% of the batch until
capped; later, sharding became our dosing instrument. The cap's failure
mode deserves a paragraph in any pretraining paper: consumed greedily, a
family's cap exhausts early and the family vanishes. Audited: 16 of 71
families extinct before 1\% of the epoch, capped-family share drifting
46.6\% $\to$ 35.7\% between first and last decile, batch shrinking
493 $\to$ 409. Late training sees a different distribution; a plausible
mechanism for late-run GIFT degradation. Fix: \emph{rationing}, a
fractional per-batch quota with carry-over. Same exposure, stationary
composition across all ten deciles, and the rationed run produced the CRPS
champion. Augmentation cannot substitute: it diversifies present samples,
not absent families.

\paragraph{Synthetic generator.}
Three measured holes: nothing between 5-minute and hourly (10T/15T degraded
on four datasets), short-series shapes structurally rejected, and no chunks
long enough for cross-resolution pairs. The generator follows KernelSynth
\citep{ansari2024chronos} via random Fourier features
\citep{rahimi2007rff}: exact GP sampling is $O(N^3)$, prohibitive at
$N{=}8192$; summing $K{=}64$ sinusoids drawn from the kernel's spectral
density is $O(NK)$ and indistinguishable by eye. Component bank: seasonal
blocks with decaying harmonics, smooth GPs, breaking trends, Student-$t$
noise 15\% of the time, level shifts, impulses, 25\% multiplicative
modulation, and a final random affine so synthetic series are not
recognizable by their normalization. Table~\ref{tab:families} lists the
five families and their targets; two have no public-data substitute at all.

\paragraph{Augmentations.}
Scale augmentation is provably inert under our normalization
(Section~\ref{sec:normalization}), so the budget goes to shape: amplitude
modulation ($p{=}0.5$), censoring at a random high quantile, same threshold
for context and target since saturation is a property of the world
($p{=}0.5$), and periodic spike trains ($p{=}0.05$; a random spike in the
target alone is unlearnable noise, a periodic one is signal). Order mirrors
physics: modulate, spike, then censor. All three are the largest single
contributor in the TiRex ablation \citep{auer2025tirex}. Historical note:
for the entire first phase the augmentation config was parsed and never
wired to the data module; every early run trained without its declared
augmentations (Appendix~\ref{app:pitfalls}).

\paragraph{The v3 corpus.}
Assembled by symlink, nothing deleted: full capped LOTSA (10.05B
observations, 4.9M chunks), Chronos-era extras, the padded short-series
block, solar\_power, the decimated block, and the synthetic families
sharded into 23 seeded files (collision with earlier corpora impossible by
seed derivation). Because share follows shard count, dose is set by
sharding: 3 unsharded families measured 11.2\%; the 23-shard allocation was
predicted at $\sim$51\% and audited at 54.0\% after removing two symlinks
from a first pass at 55.2\%. The audit is the corpus's identity card: 106
families, stationary across deciles, capped real tail 24.6\%. One
imprecision reported rather than hidden: the earlier capped corpus was
logged as both ``3--4 billion'' and ``$\sim$1.7 billion'' observations; the
exact count was never instrumented, and the scaling comparison in
Section~\ref{sec:results} states the range.
